# GAP ANALYSIS — what a recruiter-ready brand/strategy portfolio needs, and what is missing
Researched 8 Sep 2026 across ~60 sources. Every claim below is attributed. Where the evidence is
thin or the sources disagree, that is stated rather than smoothed over.

**Method note.** There is no rigorous research base for strategy portfolios. What exists is named
practitioner prescription (Mark Pollard, Rob Estreitinho, Sarah Robb, Julian Cole), awards-body
rubrics (Effie, IPA, D&AD), general web-reading research borrowed from UX (Nielsen), and the
observable behaviour of portfolios that demonstrably got people hired. Any source quoting a
"6 seconds" or "75% ATS rejection" figure is laundering a resume statistic. Discount accordingly.

numbers. A reviewer therefore leaves with the impression that you have never moved a number. You
have, repeatedly, and it is documented.

**Build:** a Proof layer. Not a stats bar of vanity tiles. A short section that says where you have
operated commercially and what moved, sourced to employer and period. It resolves the spec-heavy
slate problem in one block.

## G2. No case study states your role or contribution

**The single most universally cited rejection reason in the research.** Named independently by
Matej Latin (ADPList), Tom Scott, Mostafa Marmousa (Catchin' Talent, who calls collective language
"discomfort with ownership"), iCreatives, Semplice and AIGA.

Semplice makes **Credits & Role a mandatory named section**, not a footnote
([semplice.com](https://www.semplice.com/how-to-write-case-studies-for-your-portfolio)).

Every strategy portfolio the research verified as strong does this explicitly, in the same pattern:
a named Team block at the end of each case, with roles and **reciprocal links to collaborators**.
Casey Hall credits five people with links to their portfolios; Cai Strachan credits three with role
tags (ST / CW / AD); Katie Dunn tags collaborators (St). **The reciprocal link is the trust
mechanism** — it is cheap to write "led strategy" and expensive to link to two people who could
contradict you.

Your work index has a role column. Your five case pages have nothing. Venus Roadlines is the acute
case: real work, real collaborators (a safety supervisor, a director who approved it), and the page
does not say what you did versus what existed.

**Build:** a Role block on every case page. For spec work: "Self-initiated. Strategy, concept and
copy: Shrey Mehra." For VRL: name the actual division of labour.

## G3. No file version. Agency recruiters repackage what you send.

The most load-bearing operational finding in the research, and it comes from software documentation
rather than opinion. [Bullhorn ATS](https://kb.bullhorn.com/ats/Content/BHATS/Topics/creatingStandardResumeForCandidate.htm)
documents standard staffing behaviour: recruiters generate a "blind" version carrying **the firm's
logo and the recruiter's contact details, with the candidate's contact information stripped out.**

Joe Cooper, Head of Design & New Business at Represent, says they "send far more decks than
websites" internally, because a deck gives narrative control and can be amended per client
([Creative Lives in Progress](https://creativelivesinprogress.com/articles/portfolio-format-2026)).

**The counter-position is real:** Rachel Martinic at Artisan Talent says "if there isn't a portfolio
link, I often move on." These are not in conflict. **The link is the screening surface; the file is
what a recruiter builds to sell you onward.** You have the first and not the second.

**Build:** a deck, 2–3 cases, size-optimised, no password. Peter Larkin's constraint: "are you
making them download a 200MB file?"

# TIER 2 — Strongly evidenced. These are what separate you from the pile.

## G5. Ideas is empty, and for your specific situation it is the highest-value section on the site

I have been treating this as a nice-to-have. The research says it is closer to essential for a
strategy candidate whose slate is mostly spec.

**Camilla Petty**, writing for Open Strategy, is explicit that for a brand strategist without client
work, the calling card is not a case study at all: *"Writing is a wonderful way to ask and answer
the questions that interest you… I set wannabe strategists a writing brief… they can use that piece
as a calling card along with their CV"* ([Medium](https://medium.com/open-strategy/career-planning-for-brand-strategists-getting-into-strategy-roles-in-agencies-start-ups-and-a2754d362e8e)).

**Stanley Wood**, then design lead at Spotify, names writing as essential because it proves *"your
thinking is clear"* ([vanschneider.com](https://vanschneider.com/blog/how-to-get-a-job-at/how-to-get-a-job-at-spotify-/)).

And the observed portfolios back it. **Rhyann Clark** runs an "Obsessions" section of unsolicited
cultural analyses. **Katie Dunn** lists a One Direction Pepsi ad analysis and a creator deep-dive
alongside campaign work. **Quentin Smith** runs a Culture Journal. These are proof of *noticing*,
which is the one thing a spec campaign cannot demonstrate.

Tom Scott lists "no articles or talks" among signals that read as dated in 2026 hiring.

**Build:** three pieces. *The Time Tax* is outlined. Two more from observations you already have.
This is content only you can write, and it is now the top content priority.

## G6. No research provenance behind the insights

The strongest single structural idea surfaced by the research is **Quentin Smith's "Behind the
Insight"** ([quentinansley.com/behind-the-insight](https://quentinansley.com/behind-the-insight)) —
a second, parallel layer to the portfolio that exposes method. Five projects, each with an
expandable story naming the qualitative methods used and **the verbatim that produced the insight.**

It converts "trust me, this is an insight" into "here is where it came from." For spec work with no
results, **provenance is the substitute for outcome.**

Your cases assert strong insights. Vastr's identity-not-filing reframe, Cut The Noise's
losing-to-the-feed, AbsolutILY's unwitnessed win. None of them say where the insight came from.

**Build:** a short provenance note per case. What you read, watched, counted or noticed. It does not
need to be formal research, and pretending otherwise would fail the honesty rule. "I read every
Hungry Jack's Instagram comment for a month" is a provenance claim and a good one.

## G7. Your spec work is self-set, which is the weakest form

The research is clear that spec work is fine and misrepresentation is the problem, **but that the
strongest form of spec is a real, dated, judged brief.** Cai Strachan's Canesten case is labelled
**"D&AD Awards" at the top of the page** — the label functions as a credential, not an apology.

Live briefs exist now: [D&AD New Blood 2026](https://www.dandad.org/awards/new-blood-awards/briefs-2026)
includes Duolingo, HSBC, Twix, L'Oréal, Canesten, Nothing, Wise. You already have a Duolingo piece
and an L'Oréal piece. The One Club Young Ones and Cannes Young Lions run comparable briefs.

**Also note the seniority ceiling.** Carlos Bocai, Design Director at Base Design: concept work is
acceptable for juniors, and *"I wouldn't trust a senior designer or design director who only has
concept projects."* With six years' experience you are on the wrong side of that line if the slate
stays five-spec-two-real.

**Do:** enter one real brief this cycle and label it. It converts a self-set exercise into a dated,
externally-judged one at zero cost to the work.

## G8. No third-party validation

Two Contact blocks currently read `[GAP: LinkedIn recommendation]`. The ticker quotes are real but
anonymous, which is the weakest form.

**Avni Srivastav** solves this laterally with a homepage section titled *"STRATEGISTS x THREE WORDS
ABOUT AVNI"* — third-party testimony as a portfolio object. **Sarah Robb** recommends client
testimonials framed as before/after, plus named references willing to discuss the engagement.

**Caveat worth holding:** Burnett Specialists notes recruiters weight actual references far above
LinkedIn recommendations, and that recommendations from people unrelated to your work history carry
little. So: two attributed quotes from people who managed or worked with you beats six anonymous
ones.

**On employer logos** — you were an employee, not an agency serving clients. Attorneys on Avvo note
the test is likelihood of confusion, and that a logo wall implying client relationships is exactly
the risk. **Your existing decision to set employer names as type rather than logos is correct and
research-backed.** Keep it.

# TIER 3 — Real, cheaper, do after the above

## G9. Case studies have no above-the-fold summary

Nielsen's scroll research: **80.3% of viewing time is spent above the fold, 19.7% below**
([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention-original-research/)). Reading
follows ~25 seconds fixed plus 4.4 seconds per additional 100 words, meaning roughly 18% of anything
you add gets read ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-little-do-users-read/)).

Your Vastr case runs well over 2,000 words with the reframe arriving in section two. Katie Dunn's
Cholula case opens with a literal **"TL;DR"** heading.

**Note the disagreement honestly.** Pollard says 200 words per case. Semplice says three minutes.
Every strong strategy case actually observed runs 1,000–1,200, and Rhyann Clark's Coke Zero runs
3,500. My read: the 200-word rule is really a rule about the top of the page, and depth below it is
fine for the reader already sold. Your length is defensible; **your top-of-page is not.**

**Build:** a 40-word standfirst per case carrying the reframe, above everything else.

## G10. `shreymehra.com` is not registered

The honest argument is not that a custom domain "signals professionalism" — that is folk wisdom,
asserted everywhere and evidenced nowhere. It is mechanical: given that **44% of sourced hires now
come from re-finding someone in an existing database** and referrals convert 7% of applicants into
72% of interviews ([Pin](https://www.pin.com/blog/recruitment-statistics/), Zippia), your site's
real job is **winning the search for your own name and surviving being forwarded.** A domain you own
is what ranks, stays stable across platform migrations, and does not break when a host rebrands.

Set a renewal reminder. Domain expiry takes a site offline silently.

## G11. No interview presentation format prepared

65% of candidates report presenting portfolio-style work in interviews. HubSpot's format is a single
30-minute deep dive on **one** project. Holloway's is ~1 hour, ~6 projects built, **2–3 presented**.

What HubSpot actually wants is not a process tour: core problem–solution fit, **why alternatives were
rejected**, hard tradeoffs, where you pushed back successfully, and **design decisions you were not
proud of** ([product.hubspot.com](https://product.hubspot.com/blog/portfolio-reviews-done-right)).
Stefan Klocek's framing: *"what you fought for, what battles you lost, and why."*

**Prepare:** for each of your top three cases, a pre-worked answer to "what did you fight for and
lose." None of your cases currently contain a defeat.

## G12. LinkedIn is the discovery layer and the site is the conversion layer

LinkedIn messages reply at **17.08% vs 4.96% for automated email**; email plus LinkedIn together
converts 45.76% vs 19.73% for email alone ([Pin, 4M+ messages](https://www.pin.com/blog/recruiting-outreach-benchmark-report/)).
Arrow reversed — that is recruiters messaging candidates — but the channel asymmetry holds.

Jeremy Carson's point on title literalism is worth acting on: *"If someone's looking for an Art
Director, they search 'Art Director.' And guess who will NOT show up: the 'Creative Maker.'"*
Your headline should contain the words people search.

# WHAT YOU ALREADY HAVE THAT THE RESEARCH SAYS IS RIGHT

Worth stating, because several of these are things most candidates get wrong.

- **Spec disclosed in the first line.** Stephanie Jade Howe names this exact convention: disclosure
  at the top, in the same visual weight as a client name.
- **Employer names as type, not a logo wall.** Legally cleaner and reads as more confident.
- **No contact form.** Adobe Portfolio forms silently failed to deliver for 21 months across
  multiple threads with no root cause found. A form is an undetectable single point of failure.
  A visible email cannot fail this way. Yours is a mailto.
- **Seven pieces with three visible.** Sits inside the 3–5 visible / 6–8 total range every source
  converges on.
- **The honesty policy.** Effie rewards "honesty, authenticity and simplicity"; IPA warns against
  "Byron Sharp Bingo." Your no-invented-numbers rule is a differentiator, not a limitation.
- **A distinctive, authored visual system.** Figma's own Brand Designer posting asks for work that
  is *"culturally aware, emotionally resonant, and authored rather than overly systematized or
  generic"* ([Greenhouse](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/figma/jobs/5992087004)). Note what that
  posting does not ask for: awards, client rosters, campaign metrics.
