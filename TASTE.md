# TASTE — what the references are for
Principles extracted from Shrey's reference set. Direction, not template.
Every entry says what to take and what to leave, because a reference without a boundary
gets absorbed whole and drags the design toward someone else's system.

---

## Antony Raphy — butter ground, halftone portrait, oversized name
**Take:** the name at a scale that overlaps the photograph instead of sitting beside it, in two
colour states so the overlap reads as deliberate. Mono metadata parked in the corners, doing
quiet work. A ground colour that is not white and not grey.
**Leave:** the dotted background grid, the specific yellow, the corner-bracket menu.
**Why it works:** the photo and the name occupy the same space, so identity and person arrive
as one object rather than a portrait with a caption. That is the move we use in the hero.

## Prasshanna — black diagonal bands, mixed display faces, starburst badge
**Take:** bands that cross the full width at an angle and stack with overlap, each carrying
different content. A found badge used as punctuation, once.
**Leave:** the rainbow logo wall, the running clock, the language switcher.
**Why it works:** the diagonal breaks a page that would otherwise be a column of rectangles,
and it carries real content while doing it. Decoration that is also information.

## The orange collage — cut type, barcodes, leader lines, boxed ID labels
**Take:** annotation as a visual device. A leader line from a detail to a label is how you show
thinking without writing a paragraph about it. Found type keeps its original ink.
**Leave:** the flat orange field, the ID-card frame, the density.
**Why it works:** it looks like someone marked up their own work. That is the exact impression
a case study wants, and it is why case pages get leader lines rather than more body copy.

## The named references, translated

**Vogue** — the editorial spread. Text and image are composed together on a grid, not stacked.
A page has a top-left entry point and an eye path that was decided by someone.

**A24** — restraint with one loud moment. Most of the frame is quiet so the one bold thing lands.
This is where the single-accent rule comes from.

**Uber, Apple** — clarity as a form of respect. Nothing is decorative that could be structural.
Take the discipline, not the neutrality: neither of them has a personality we want.

**JoJo's Bizarre Adventure** — confidence in strangeness. Bold colour, unusual poses,
committed choices held long enough to become style rather than accident.
Take the willingness to be odd on purpose. Leave the literal.

---

## The through line

Every reference above is doing the same thing: **composition carrying the interest**, so effects
do not have to. Type, grid, whitespace, scale, contrast and rhythm are the tools. Shadows,
gradients, glass and rounded cards are what a page reaches for when the composition is not working.

## The test
Take any screen and ask: if you removed every effect, would the layout still be interesting?
If not, the effects are load-bearing and the composition has not been done yet.

## The failure the references guard against
Not ugliness. Genericness. A page can be well made, correctly spaced, accessible and
completely forgettable. The references exist to keep specificity in the work, because the
one impression that undoes everything is looking like it could belong to anyone.
