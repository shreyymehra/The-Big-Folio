A Brand World
From Scratch

L’Oréal owns hair. It does not own the scalp underneath it — and there are millions of people who have one without the other. A new line, built for them, without making anyone else feel evicted.

Lead: Brand Support: Creative Strategy Spec 5 min read
01 / Problem

When you think hair, you think L’Oréal. Is that sentiment shared by bald people?

Probably not. The brand is built around a keratin-powered, long-haired audience, and the further you sit from that picture the less the category appears to be speaking to you at all.

But bald people are not one audience. For some it was a choice. For others it was a decision taken for them. Those are two completely different emotional starting points and the category treats them as one demographic, if it addresses them at all.

There is a second group nobody is serving either: people who still have hair and are quietly anxious about losing it. Today they are only sold prevention, which is a product category that speaks entirely in the language of fear.

02 / Hypothesis

You might not need hair for haircare after all.

The scalp does not disappear when the hair does. It still burns, still dries, still ages. The biological need persists perfectly intact while the category stops speaking the moment the hair goes — which means the exclusion is a marketing decision, not a product one.

So the bet: build one line that serves both anxieties at once. The person who wants to make baldness look deliberate, and the person watching their hairline and dreading it. Both want the same thing, which is to feel like themselves. If L’Oréal can say that credibly, it stops being a hair company and becomes a confidence one.

03 / Approach

Start with what L’Oréal already owns, then argue the gap.

  • i.Audit the portfolio honestly. Kérastase Genesis, CeraVe and Elvive already carry real scalp credentials. The gap is not capability — it is that none of it is positioned for a head without hair on it.
  • ii.Fix the competitive frame. Head & Shoulders is a scalp-first brand and has been for decades, so it is a benchmark, not a foil. The honest white space sits against Bold and MANTL: right idea, no distribution, no household trust.
  • iii.Separate the two audiences in the messaging without separating the product line.
  • iv.Write the launch as an extension, not a pivot. Nobody currently buying should feel replaced.

Positioning

For people who do not let their hair define their worth but still want to feel their best self, L’Oréal is the one hair and scalp care brand that delivers complete skin, scalp and hair benefits — because care was never actually about the hair.

The line that leads

“Hair or not, here we come.”

Supporting: “Healthy hair or shiny head, we’re there for what you have.” and “No matter what, it’s always L’Oréal.”

Long form: “What if one day you ran your hand through your hair and found nothing? Well — that can’t be true. You’ll always find L’Oréal there.”

04 / Result

What exists now.

A positioning statement, a corrected competitive set, a launch platform and a copy system across three tones. Nothing has been tested and nothing shipped, so there are no numbers here and I am not going to invent any.

What the piece is meant to prove is narrower and more useful: I can find a commercially real gap inside a brand this size, check it against the portfolio that already exists, and build the argument without flattering myself with fake results.

Paste artwork 03 — launch platform
05 / Learning

My first competitive set was wrong. I had Head & Shoulders as a hair-only foil when it has been scalp-first for decades — a mistake any FMCG interviewer catches in one question. Check the category before you claim the gap.
