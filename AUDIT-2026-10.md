# AUDIT — UI/UX, copy, and career-track fit

Audited against the running `index.html` and the five case pages, 6 Oct 2026,
with `PORTFOLIO-SUMMARY.md` and `FILL-IN.md` as the stated intent.

**Nothing already tracked in FILL-IN is repeated here as a finding.** The six
quotes, the colour photograph, the first Ideas post, the Venus tag, the resume
format and the two live URLs are logged and correctly prioritised. This document
is only what those lists do not cover.

Seven findings. Two are serious.

---

## 1. THE HEADLINE PROBLEM: the hero sells a track the work does not evidence

The hero, the nav, and the skills ticker all lead with **Product marketing**.
The work slate contains no product marketing.

| Track claimed | Artefacts on the site | State |
|---|---|---|
| Product marketing | none | — |
| GTM | Vastr one-page GTM brief | spec |
| Brand strategy | Vastr, Cut The Noise, AbsolutILY, L'Oreal, Duolingo | 3 spec, 2 unbuilt |
| Comms / enablement | Venus Roadlines | shipped, real |
| AI-enabled | VoiceDNA | in build |

The ticker names **PRDs, user research, launch planning, sales enablement,
competitive analysis** as skills. Not one of the five has an artefact behind it
anywhere on the site. A product marketing hiring manager reads the hero, scans
the work, finds campaigns and brand decks, and concludes the headline is
aspirational. That is a worse read than a brand strategist who says so.

Your centre of gravity is brand and campaign. Your hero says product marketing.
One of the two has to move.

**Two ways to close it, and they are not equal.**

**Option A, cheap and honest.** Reorder the discipline line to
`Brand strategy · GTM · Product marketing`, and cut the five unevidenced skills
from the ticker. Costs nothing, ships today, and the site stops over-claiming.
It also narrows which reqs you get read for.

**Option B, expensive and correct.** Build one product marketing artefact from a
real stint: the positioning, the launch, and the enablement behind the iSelect
and Compare the Market work. This is the same write-up that has been open since
the first audit, and it now has a second and larger reason to exist. It is the
only route to leading with product marketing without the claim being hollow.

**Recommendation: do A this week and B next.** A is not a retreat; it is
accurate until B exists.

**For the AI-company track specifically.** Anthropic and OpenAI hire people who
can be read, not just seen. VoiceDNA leading the work section is exactly right,
and it is the strongest single decision on the site. The matching weakness is
that Ideas is the only section that asks to be believed rather than showing
something, which lands hardest with precisely those employers.

## 2. SERIOUS: internal issue codes are rendering on the live page

A visitor currently reads, on the homepage:

> **G02** The first post. Not written yet.
> **G03** Where the photographs, the paintings and the writing live.
> **G04** Medium and X handles. Not guessed: a 404 in front of a recruiter is
> worse than a short list.

`G01` through `G06` all appear in the rendered text. The visible-gap convention
is right and I would not drop it, but a code is a ticket number, and a ticket
number on a live page reads as an unfinished build rather than as a standard
being held. The second half of G04 is worse: it is an internal note explaining
your reasoning to yourself, addressed to "a recruiter", read by the recruiter.

**Fix.** Strip the `Gnn` prefix and the self-addressed half from anything that
renders. The slot can still be visible and still be honest:

- `G02 The first post. Not written yet.` → **"The first post is being written."**
- `G04 Medium and X handles. Not guessed: ..."` → delete the row entirely until
  the handles exist. An absent link is invisible; an explained absence is a
  paragraph about something you do not have.
- `G01 / G05 / G06 / G07 / G08` are artwork placeholders. A blank, ruled slot
  reads as a layout awaiting an image. A slot with a code in it reads as a bug.

Keep the codes in `FILL-IN.md`, where they work.

## 3. The hero is carrying nine things and a moving navigation

Counted: the photograph, the ransom-note name, the claim, the rolling line, the
discipline line, two calls to action, the caption, and six orbiting marks. Plus
two continuous motions and a colour-on-hover.

Three specific consequences.

**The claim and the rolling line are two headlines.** "I build brands people
want to belong to." and "and I have always wanted to build [brands/experiences/
campaigns...]" make the same move twice, and the second one dilutes the first by
listing six things instead of asserting one. The claim is the stronger line.
**Cut the rolling line, or cut the claim.** Keeping both is why the hero reads
busy.

**Primary navigation is in motion.** Six marks ride a carousel path. A moving
target is harder to acquire than a stationary one, and the ring competes with
the claim for the eye during the ten seconds that decide the visit. The top nav
already carries Work, Ideas, Me, Contact, so the ring is a second IA for the
same destinations plus FAQ and Listening. **Suggestion:** stop the ring on
`:hover` and `:focus-within` of the hero, or park it and animate only on entry.
The device survives; the usability problem goes.

**"Rarely in that order" is gone.** That line was the one piece of hero copy
that could not belong to anyone else, and it was the answer to the non-linear
idea you named as the driving key. The discipline line now reads as a
conventional three-item list. Worth restoring under the new triad.

## 4. Your real numbers are the only ones on the site, and they are moving

`+46%`, `−78%`, `+310%`, `160k`, `20k MAU`, `1.2M`, `130` now live inside the
ticker, correctly paired with their source. They are the most persuasive content
you own and they scroll past at reading speed, interleaved with fifteen skill
labels and three "For X" tags.

Numbers in motion are numbers not read. A scanning recruiter takes an
impression of "there are numbers" rather than any single figure.

**Fix.** Keep the ticker for the skills. Lift the seven figures into a static
band, employer named, with one line under each saying what you changed to move
it. That mechanism line is still the open item from every previous audit and it
is still the highest-value hour of writing available to you.

## 5. The work slate appears three times on one page

The homepage shows three pieces in Selected work, then lists all seven again on
the magazine's Projects leaf, then links to `/work` which lists all seven a
third time. The five process steps appear twice on the homepage and a third time
on `/about`.

Repetition is not free: on a long page it reads as padding, and it means the
magazine's most distinctive leaf is a list the visitor has already seen.

**Fix.** The magazine's Projects leaf should carry what `/work` cannot: the
thread between the pieces, in a paragraph. Why these seven, what they have in
common, what you were testing across them. That is an Ideas-section job and it
would partly retire the "Coming next" problem.

---

## 6. The magazine device over-promises

Eight leaves, page-turn animation, a contents list, an issue number. The device
announces a periodical. The content is a manifesto, a list already shown twice,
and a page that says the posts are being written.

The craft is not the problem. The ratio is. A device this elaborate makes the
emptiness louder than a plain section would.

**Fix, in order of cost.** Either publish one post, which is already G02 and the
right answer, or reduce the issue to the leaves that have content and drop the
contents page until there are enough leaves to need one. A four-leaf zine that
is full beats an eight-leaf magazine that is half empty.

## 7. Copy: four lines that work against you

Flagged, not changed. These are yours.

**"I am too young to have dead set opinions."** Manifesto, belief five. In a
room this reads as modesty. On a page read by someone deciding whether you can
own a positioning call, it reads as hedging, and it sits immediately after four
beliefs that are confident. It is also the only one of the five that is about
you rather than about the work. Consider replacing it with a belief you would
defend.

**"saved their company more than 200K AUD by finding a legal loophole."** Me
section. "Legal loophole" is doing you no favours with a corporate reader, who
hears rule-bending rather than resourcefulness. The achievement is real and the
framing is free. "a clause nobody had read" or "a contractual term everyone had
missed" lands the same story without the flinch.

**"The briefs nobody asked me to write."** Used as the headline for both the
homepage work section and `/work`. It is a very good line and it is doing a
quiet disservice: it frames the entire slate as unrequested, which is true of
the spec pieces and false of Venus Roadlines, the one real shipped project. The
`/work` page can keep it. The homepage section, which leads with VoiceDNA and
Venus Roadlines, needs a line that does not call them self-set.

**"Worked with"** over the logo wall. FILL-IN frames the BCG and UN Youth
decision as a binary: the "College project" line goes back on, or it does not.
There is a third option that is more precise than either. Replace the label with
**"Employers, clients and volunteer work"**. It is accurate for all eight marks,
it removes the implication without the self-diminishing phrase, and a recruiter
who then finds a university society finds exactly what the label said.

## CHANGES TO `PORTFOLIO-SUMMARY.md`

The document is accurate and unusually well written. Four additions, no
rewrites.

1. **Add a career-track map, as its own section after "The system, in one
   page".** The summary documents what is on each tab and never says which job
   each piece is evidence for. That mapping is the thing a reader of this
   document most needs and the thing its absence hides, which is finding 1. The
   table in §1 above can go in verbatim.

2. **The "Ground" and "Colour" paragraphs give values with no contrast
   numbers.** Every other claim in the document is specific. State the measured
   ratios for cape blue and cape red on the off-white, and the small-type cutoff
   for red, the way the colour rules are actually enforced. A future session will
   otherwise re-derive them or guess.

3. **"What I would fix next" is five items and omits the two largest.** The
   track mismatch in §1 and the mechanism lines under the numbers both outrank
   everything currently on that list. Suggested order: track mismatch, mechanism
   lines, names on the quotes, the Gnn codes, the colour photograph, one Ideas
   post, the Venus tag.

4. **Add a "What this does not claim" paragraph.** The document is going to be
   read by sessions that will act on it. It should say, in its own voice, that
   five of seven pieces are spec, that one is real and shipped, that one is in
   build, and that no product marketing artefact exists yet. Writing the weakness
   into the locked summary is what stops the next session from quietly papering
   over it.

## WHAT IS WORKING, AND SHOULD NOT BE TOUCHED

- **Leading the work section with VoiceDNA.** The only piece that is a product
  rather than a document about one, in front of the employers you are targeting.
- **B2B / B2C in a plane on every `/work` row.** This is how case studies
  actually get sorted before they are read, and almost nobody does it.
- **Three filters over seven pieces instead of six.** Correct reasoning,
  correctly applied.
- **Key decisions with a trade-off on each.** The trade-off line is what
  separates a case study from a brochure, and these pages have it.
- **Spec disclosed in the first line, every time.** A credibility asset.
- **Fluent / Working knowledge instead of skill bars.** More honest and reads
  better to anyone who has run an interview.
- **"Enough about the work" sitting on the rule.** The single best typographic
  decision on the site.
