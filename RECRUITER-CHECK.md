# RECRUITER-CHECK.md

A sceptical first-time reader, arrived from a cold email, ten seconds of
patience. What survives, what gets cut, and what is currently working against
you. Nothing here is applied to your copy except the seven mechanical fixes in
§1, because copy you wrote is source material and cuts are yours to approve.

---

## 1. FIXED ALREADY (mechanical, no voice change)

1. **89 em dashes removed** across all ten pages. Replaced with full stops,
   commas, colons or middots, never reworded. One sentence got mangled in the
   sweep and was repaired by hand (Vastr, "It plays on *vast*").
2. **Section numbers collided.** Proof and Ideas were both `02`. The page now
   runs 01 Work, 02 Proof, 03 Ideas, 04 How the work happens, 05 About,
   06 Contact.
3. **The hero doors advertised 01 / 03 / 05** against a page numbered 01 to 06.
   They now point to the real numbers.
4. **The Proof lede said "the seven briefs below"** while sitting underneath
   the work section. Now "above".
5. **A stray comma on work card 07**, left by the em dash sweep. Now a middot.
6. **Nav collided with the section eyebrow on case pages.** The nav is now a
   solid band and anchors clear it.
7. **Case pages moved to the quiet skin.** Near-white reading ground, slate
   type, clay accent that carries small type at 5.24 where fluoro failed at
   3.45.

## 2. THE BIGGEST PROBLEM: you describe your discipline three different ways

On one page a recruiter reads:

- Loader: `Brand · Marketing · GTM`
- Hero: `Brand · Product · GTM · Creative`
- Ticker: `Brand Strategy · Creative Direction · Communications · Copywriting ·
  Campaign Planning · AI Workflow Design · Community · Positioning`

Three lists, three different answers, none of them wrong. A reader trying to
work out which req to put you against gets no help from any of them, and the
eight-item version actively hurts: a list that long reads as a person who has
not decided.

**Recommendation.** One list, used everywhere, four items maximum. The hero
version is the right one. Delete the loader list and cut the ticker strip to
the same four, or delete the strip. **Needs your call.**

## 3. THE CLAIM THAT BREAKS ON CLICK

The hero says **Seven briefs**. `/work/` shows seven cards. Two of them have
no case page: 06 is labelled "Case page not built", 07 "In development".

A recruiter who clicks either one learns that two sevenths of the evidence does
not exist. That is a worse outcome than never claiming seven.

**Recommendation.** Say **Five briefs** in the hero and move 06 and 07 to a
plain text line at the foot of `/work/` under "In development". The number goes
down, the credibility goes up, and hard rule 4 stops being broken.

## 4. WORDS DOING NO WORK

Ranked by how much a cut buys you.

| Where | Now | Cut to | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brain dump intro | "Knowing what to do and how to do it is 80% of it. Here are the five steps that get me there: the process, not the theory of it." | "Five steps. The process, not the theory of it." | The 80% line is repeated verbatim in step 01, nine words later. |
| Step 01 | 61 words to say "listen" | ~25 | Three sentences make the same point. "Take notes like it matters" is the one worth keeping. |
| Step 02 | "No preconceptions. No references that anchor me to someone else's answer. I approach every brief as if I've heard nothing relevant about the space, which lets me bring perspective from scratch." | Keep the first two sentences and the last line. | The middle sentence restates the first two. |
| Work section sub | "Three pieces held in one frame. Hover one to look inside." | "Hover to look inside." | The first half describes the layout the reader can already see. |
| `/work/` intro | "Five I set myself, two that shipped. Spec work says so in its first line, every time, and if a number isn't real, it isn't here." | Two sentences, split them. | Good content, one clause too long to land in a scan. |
| Ticker | Eight disciplines | Four, or delete | See §2. |

**A note on what NOT to cut.** "The briefs nobody asked me to write" and
"Rarely in that order" are the two lines on the site that could not belong to
anyone else. Everything above is about clearing space around them.

## 5. WHAT A RECRUITER STILL CANNOT FIND

Ordered by how often it costs you the callback.

1. **What moved the numbers.** Proof carries seven real figures and not one
   mechanism. This is the single highest-value hour of writing available to
   you, and it has been open since the first audit.
2. **Who said the six quotes.** Six unattributed compliments read as invented,
   which is worse than having none. A first name, role and company against two
   of them carries the whole section.
3. **A resume.** The link exists and points at nothing.
4. **Any third-party validation.** No recommendations, no named referees.
5. **Ideas.** Three empty slots on a live page, in a section your own brief
   calls the intellectual layer.

Items 1, 2 and 4 are all the same problem wearing different clothes: the site
asks to be believed and gives a reader nothing to check.

## 6. WHAT IS WORKING

Worth saying, because the list above is long.

- The ten-second test passes. Name, discipline, and a claim that names a number
  are all above the fold.
- Spec is disclosed in the first line of every case, every time. Most
  portfolios hide this. Disclosing it is a credibility asset, not a liability.
- The `[GAP]` blocks are the right call. A visible hole reads as standards; a
  quietly missing section reads as an unfinished site.
- Venus Roadlines is the strongest page you have, and it is the least
  conventionally impressive one. A 106-page bilingual manual with named credits
  beats a beautiful spec deck in front of anyone senior.
