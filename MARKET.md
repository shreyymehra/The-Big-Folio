# MARKET — what this portfolio is competing against, and what to add

Written 23 Sep 2026. Sources at the end. Everything here is checked, not assumed.

---

## 1. The market is two markets, and only one of them is crowded

**Wall of Portfolios**, the directory four of the Reference Book links point into, carries roughly
1,770 portfolios in five categories:

| Category | Count | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Minimalist | 810+ | ~46% |
| Dark themed | 390+ | ~22% |
| Interactive | 300+ | ~17% |
| Creative | 180+ | ~10% |
| Modern layout | 90+ | ~5% |

Two things follow.

**One:** that is a *product and UX designer* market. Every featured profile reads Product Designer,
Senior Product Designer, Motion Designer, Visual Designer. Shrey is not competing in it. It is a
taste benchmark, not a competitive set, and it should be read that way.

**Two:** in the market he *is* competing in, product marketing and GTM, a built portfolio is rare.
The standard artefact is a LinkedIn profile and a deck sent on request. This is the actual
advantage, and it is worth stating plainly: among product designers a portfolio like this is table
stakes; among PMM candidates it is unusual enough to be the reason for the interview.

The risk that comes with it is the mirror image. A PMM portfolio that looks like a designer's
portfolio invites the question *is this person a marketer or a designer?* — which is the exact
ambiguity the positioning line already fights ("Not a designer. Not a copywriter.").

## 2. The rubric the work is actually graded against

From Anthropic's current Product Marketing Manager posting, which is the sharpest available
statement of what the target employers want. Minimum qualifications, condensed but not softened:

1. Proven **positioning and messaging ownership** for established software used across personas
2. Strong **writing and storytelling**, adaptable across formats
3. **ICP and persona frameworks** that scale across channels
4. **Sales enablement assets**, built with the sales team
5. **Autonomous IC**, shipping assets directly
6. Systems thinking, conviction in a changing environment
7. **Well-versed in using AI, namely Claude, to inform and automate PMM workflows**
8. Experience marketing **AI or productivity software** to cross-functional buyers

Preferred: analyst relations, plans and pricing.

The responsibilities read the same way: *define the story*, *give sellers the conviction to close*,
*equip buyers*, *make the message land in their language*, *turn scattered wins into a playbook*.

### How the portfolio scores against it, honestly

| Rubric line | Evidence on the site today | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Positioning and messaging ownership | Vastr (spec, blank page), VoiceDNA positioning | **Partial.** Nothing on established software with real personas |
| Writing and storytelling | The whole site, and the case bodies | **Strong.** The site is the artefact |
| ICP and persona frameworks | Vastr's GTM brief names an audience | **Weak.** Never framed as a framework |
| Sales enablement | Nothing labelled as such | **Absent**, but see below |
| Autonomous IC | Solo on everything except VRL | **Strong**, and provable |
| Systems thinking | VRL's annexure system, the tagline system | **Strong**, under-labelled |
| Using AI to automate PMM work | VoiceDNA, built on the Claude API | **Strongest asset on the site, buried** |
| Marketing AI or productivity software | VoiceDNA | **Partial**, not live |

Two findings matter more than the rest.

**VoiceDNA is the highest-leverage page and it is the thinnest.** A tool that diagnoses brand voice
gaps, built solo on the Claude API, is a literal demonstration of rubric line 7 and a claim to
line 8. It currently runs four minutes, has no live link, no screenshot and one open `[GAP]`. For
the target employers this page is worth more than the two campaign cases combined.

**The slate over-indexes on advertising craft.** Cut The Noise and AbsolutILY are the two most
enjoyable pieces on the site and the two least aligned with the rubric: they demonstrate campaign
idea and copy, not positioning, enablement or GTM ownership. They should stay, because personality
is the reason a stranger keeps reading, but they should not lead.

## 3. What to add, in priority order

Each item says what it proves against the rubric. Nothing here is a redesign.

### Tier 1 — closes a rubric gap

1. **A live VoiceDNA, or a working demo of it.** One screen a visitor can use beats every page of
   reasoning on the site. If it cannot ship, a 20-second capture of a real gap analysis running,
   with the input and output visible, does most of the job. *Proves: 7, 8, 5.*
2. **A messaging artefact, shown.** One positioning statement or messaging matrix, real or from
   iSelect / Compare the Market work, with a slice annotated. The rubric asks for the artefact
   type by name and the site currently describes messaging without ever showing one.
   *Proves: 1, 3.*
3. **Reframe VRL as enablement, because that is what it was.** A 106-page bilingual manual written
   so a buyer (Tata Steel) could assess a company and a warehouse worker could use it, is a sales
   enablement asset and a persona-split document. The page tells the story; the label is missing.
   *Proves: 3, 4, 6.*
4. **An "influenced vs controlled" line on every real number.** iSelect +46% MoM, vendor costs
   −78%, Tickertape CTR +310%. The Medium reference and every senior interviewer ask the same
   question: what did you own. Say it before they ask. *Proves: credibility.*

### Tier 2 — matches the taste of the top of the market

5. **Audience and type labels on every work row**, the way the case-study wall labels its own:
   `B2B · Positioning`, `B2C · Campaign`, `B2B · Enablement`. Scannable in a second, and it makes
   the slate read as a body of work rather than a list of projects.
6. **Cut the slate from seven to five on `/work`.** The Medium piece is blunt about it: more is
   not better, sharper is better. Duolingo is in development, L'Oréal is unbuilt. Show five and
   the average goes up.
7. **A one-line credential strip under the hero**, in McLane Teitel's form: company, role, years,
   four rows. The site currently makes a visitor scroll to Me before they learn he has worked with
   anyone. *iSelect, Compare the Market, Tickertape, TEDxVIPS, Bliss & Birch, Amazon.*
8. **Three named threads on Me**, Harrison Wheeler's structure: a heading plus one short paragraph
   each, replacing the five undifferentiated paragraphs. He has the threads already.

### Tier 3 — personality, once the above is done

9. The work card fan, the leader lines, the globe, the bobblehead. All specified in `MOTION.md`,
   all blocked on assets rather than decisions.

### Explicitly not recommended

- **A services or hire-me funnel.** Ruled out already, and the rubric does not reward it.
- **More spec campaign work.** The two on the site already carry that register.
- **Dark mode as a selling point.** 390 portfolios on that wall are dark themed. It is a courtesy,
  not a differentiator, and it already works.
- **Chasing the minimalist category.** 810 portfolios are there. The whole argument of this site is
  that it is not.
