Voice’D

I’m building a tool that tells you how your brand actually sounds versus how you think it does.

Constraint
A solo build, so the reasoning had to come from a general model rather than a trained classifier.
State
In build. Not live, and not linked until it is.
Problem

The most discussed element of brand strategy, and the least measurable

Companies run workshops, write guidelines, produce a document that lives in a shared drive, and then the content, or three different agencies, produces work that sounds like several different people. The gap between the stated voice and the actual voice is real, persistent and expensive. Nobody has a fast diagnostic for it.

What does your brand actually sound like, right now, in this piece of content? Not what the guidelines say it should sound like. What it does.
The build

React, Claude API, Vercel

The choice of Claude specifically was deliberate. This is not a sentiment tool. Positive, negative, neutral is a blunt instrument for a nuanced problem. Voice characterisation requires reasoning across register, rhythm, specificity level, formality, warmth and authority simultaneously. A custom-trained classifier for a solo build would not do that.

The build was motivated by a pattern observed across client work: brands with strong guidelines keep producing content that misses them. Not because the team doesn't understand the guidelines, because there is no feedback loop between production and intent.

Key decisions

Three calls, and what each one cost

A tool is a series of refusals. These are the three that shaped what it does and does not do.

Decision 01

A reasoning model, not a sentiment classifier

Options
Sentiment analysis · a custom-trained classifier · the Claude API
Why
This is not a sentiment problem. Positive, negative, neutral is a blunt instrument here. Voice characterisation means reasoning across register, rhythm, specificity, formality, warmth and authority at once, and a custom-trained classifier for a solo build would not do that.
Trade-off
Every run costs money and takes time, which a local classifier would not. The output also has to be held to a fixed shape, or two runs cannot be compared to each other.
Decision 02

A gap, not a score

Options
A single voice score · a gap map between stated and actual
Why
Stated voice and actual voice are placed side by side, and the space between them is the brief for the edit. A number tells a team they have a problem. The gap tells them where it starts.
Trade-off
A score travels on its own and gets screenshotted into a deck. A gap map has to be read. It trades shareability for the thing that is actually useful in the room.
Decision 03

The brand has to say what it is aiming for

Options
Infer the intended voice from the content · require guidelines, reference examples or stated principles as input
Why
A tool that guesses the intent and then measures against its own guess is measuring nothing. The diagnostic only means something if the target came from the brand.
Trade-off
It will not work for a brand that has never written its voice down, which is a real share of them. The friction is the price of the answer meaning anything.
What it does

Makes the gap visible

Input a piece of content. Input how the brand says it should sound: guidelines, reference examples, stated principles. Voice’D returns a gap analysis across voice dimensions: what's landing, what's drifting, and where the intervention starts.

The output is visual. The brand's stated voice and the content's actual voice are placed side by side. The space between them is the brief for the edit.

This page shows the shape of the answer, not an answer. A captured run and the live link follow when it deploys.

Learning

Most brands don't know what their voice sounds like

They know what they want it to sound like. The gap between aspiration and reality is a product, and it is also the most honest brief a brand strategist can work from.

The secondary insight: a tool that makes the gap visible changes the conversation from “this doesn't feel right” to “here is specifically what is off and here is where it starts.” That shift from intuition to evidence makes client relationships faster and more productive.

Once it ships, this will be the only page on this portfolio where a visitor can see something working, not just read about it. The rest of the work here is thinking made visible. This is thinking made functional.
