VoiceDNA

I built a tool that tells you how your brand actually sounds versus how you think it does.

4 min readShrey Mehra

Real work. Built, shipped, live on Vercel.

VOICEDNA
Problem

The most discussed element of brand strategy, and the least measurable

Companies run workshops, write guidelines, produce a document that lives in a shared drive — and then the content, or three different agencies, produces work that sounds like several different people. The gap between the stated voice and the actual voice is real, persistent and expensive. Nobody has a fast diagnostic for it.

What does your brand actually sound like, right now, in this piece of content? Not what the guidelines say it should sound like. What it does.
The build

React, Claude API, Vercel

The choice of Claude specifically was deliberate. This is not a sentiment tool — positive, negative, neutral is a blunt instrument for a nuanced problem. Voice characterisation requires reasoning across register, rhythm, specificity level, formality, warmth and authority simultaneously. A custom-trained classifier for a solo build would not do that.

The build was motivated by a pattern observed across client work: brands with strong guidelines keep producing content that misses them. Not because the team doesn't understand the guidelines — because there is no feedback loop between production and intent.

What it does

Makes the gap visible

Input a piece of content. Input how the brand says it should sound — guidelines, reference examples, stated principles. VoiceDNA returns a gap analysis across voice dimensions: what's landing, what's drifting, and where the intervention starts.

The output is visual. The brand's stated voice and the content's actual voice are placed side by side. The space between them is the brief for the edit.

Learning

Most brands don't know what their voice sounds like

They know what they want it to sound like. The gap between aspiration and reality is a product — and it is also the most honest brief a brand strategist can work from.

The secondary insight: a tool that makes the gap visible changes the conversation from “this doesn't feel right” to “here is specifically what is off and here is where it starts.” That shift from intuition to evidence makes client relationships faster and more productive.

This is also the only page on this portfolio where a visitor can see something working, not just read about it. The rest of the work here is thinking made visible. This is thinking made functional.
