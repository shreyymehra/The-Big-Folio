Vastr: Out With The Old

An AI wardrobe companion for people who want to dress like themselves — without the 7am spiral.

7 min readShrey Mehra

Spec work — disclosed here, once. Vastr is fictional; the thinking is not.

VASTR
Problem

The wardrobe problem was never volume

People own more clothes than any generation before them and still stand in front of a full closet with nothing to wear. The decision fatigue is real, the waste is compounding, and the apps built to solve it have all made the same mistake: they treat the wardrobe as a management problem rather than an identity one.

You don't need a catalogue. You need a system that understands you well enough to dress you — from what you already own, in a way that actually feels like you.

Hypothesis

The AI's job is not to have taste

If an app could catalogue a wardrobe intelligently — not manually, not laboriously — and return outfits that reflect the user's own aesthetic rather than a generic suggested style, it would solve decision fatigue without the implicit insult of telling someone who they should be.

The AI's job is not to have taste. It's to surface yours.
The name

Vastr

Vastr comes from the Sanskrit for clothing — ancient, precise, culturally rooted without being exclusive. It plays on vast, because the problem is never scarcity. It's that what people own feels unnavigable.

Two names were rejected before this one. Debra wanted to be a voice assistant — warm in the wrong direction for a utility tool. Whear (what to wear) was clever in a room and impossible to spell from hearing; a poor-recall name is a marketing problem baked in on day one. Vastr leaves room to grow beyond any single function.

The product

Three features, one loop

Digital Wardrobe

Catalogue what you own via product tag, description, or photo. Vastr sorts, remembers and surfaces it. No manual upkeep.

Outfit Engine

Input a vibe, an occasion, or a mood. Vastr returns outfits built from what is already in the wardrobe, styled in your visual language — not an influencer's.

The Shop

If something is genuinely missing, the shop finds it. Compare options and buy through the affiliate layer. The product earns only when the user decides to add something. Not when they scroll.

The brand

A calm wardrobe on a good morning

Logotype: the V is a clothing hanger. The crossbar of the letter is the rail — functional, precise, works at any scale from favicon to billboard.

Palette: blue, white, indigo. Not fashion-black, not tech-blue. The visual register of a calm wardrobe on a good morning.

Tone: JARVIS from Iron Man. Functional, precise, occasionally dry. Talks to you like it knows what it is doing and respects that you do too. Never over-explains. If Vastr says “here's your Tuesday,” it means it.

Messaging

Value-Based Clothing

Comfort of Choice

The decision is already made. A good one. Without the Sunday-night wardrobe spiral.

Utility & Time-Savvy

Your wardrobe works as hard as you do. Everything you own earns its place or gets surfaced until it does.

Your Style, Big-Time

Not someone else's aesthetic at your scale. Yours, louder.

The launch

Teaser, then live

The teaser shows a grey, identical world. Same white tee. Same blue jeans. No variation, no identity, no decision — just a directive carried out. One voice, plain: “I own 47 things and have nothing to wear.” No product. Just the problem, named.

Launch: the same wardrobe. Vastr in it now. Pieces surface. An outfit assembles that is unmistakably that person — not a suggestion, a reflection.

Out with the old — actually, no. In with the old, better.
GTM brief

The one-page version

Objective. 10,000 app downloads in the first 30 days from 18–30s in Tier 1 and Tier 2 cities. A 5,000-strong waitlist before launch day. Affiliate click-through above 8% within 60 days of the shop going live.

Audience. 18–30, style-aware, time-poor, decision-fatigued. They own more than they wear. They would dress better if they could find what they already have.

Positioning. For people who want to dress intentionally without the cognitive load, Vastr is the AI wardrobe companion that catalogues what you own and builds outfits that feel like you — unlike Whering, Indyx, Acloset, Save Your Wardrobe and Stylebook, which require manual effort without intelligent output.

Revenue. Affiliate pricing on shop purchases. The product earns when the user chooses to buy something they genuinely need — not on subscription, not on scroll volume.

Channels. TikTok for transformation content and creator seeding · Instagram for visual identity and waitlist Stories · App Store and Play Store keyword SEO · PR on the sustainability and AI angle · email for waitlist nurture and post-download onboarding.

[GAP: benchmark reasoning for the three targets — 65% outfit acceptance, 40% day-30 return, 8% affiliate CTR]
Result

No numbers, because there are none

Spec, so there are no post-launch numbers — and no invented ones. What would be measured: downloads at day 7 and day 30, wardrobe entries per user, outfit acceptance rate, day-30 return rate, affiliate click-through.

A 65%+ acceptance rate is the signal that the AI is reflecting the user's taste rather than guessing at it. That is the only number that would tell you whether the hypothesis held.

Learning

Every funded wardrobe app built the same wrong incentive

The product earns when you use it more and buy more — which means the interest of the product and the interest of the user are misaligned from day one. Vastr's affiliate model fires only on a genuine purchase decision. The product has to earn the moment someone decides they are missing something, not manufacture that moment.

The second thing building this revealed: AI in fashion has been positioned almost entirely around trend and recommendation — what you should want next. The more useful question, and the less crowded space, is what you already have and what you can do with it. Sustainability as a byproduct of utility is a stronger position than sustainability as a brief.
